About

Bridging Learning and Practice

I am a strategic educational leader with over 10 years of experience in experiential learning, professional development, and career education across post-secondary and community sectors. I love building impactful partnerships and developing innovative programs that bridge learning and practice. My expertise spans program development, coordination, facilitation, strategic planning, and instructional design, and I am committed to equitable, accessible, and transformative learning opportunities.

My work supports and mobilizes emerging leaders to pursue meaningful careers and make impactful contributions in their communities and institutions. Today, I lead graduate professional development at Western University, helping doctoral and graduate students recognize the value of their skills for careers within and beyond academia. My background in adult education and community development, and my doctoral research in educational leadership, shape how I design programs.

  • Based in: London, Ontario, Canada
  • Current role: Graduate Professional Development Coordinator, Western University
  • Education: EdD, Educational Leadership (Western University); M.Ed., Adult Education and Community Development (University of Toronto)

Research

Research & Publications

Scholarship on doctoral career development and student engagement in Canadian higher education.

2025 · Western University

Mobilizing Doctoral Students for Impactful Careers Beyond Academia

My doctoral dissertation-in-practice examines how to increase doctoral student engagement with non-academic career development. It frames career development as a sustainable development concern, connecting students' skills to the UN Sustainable Development Goals, and introduces the CARES framework to guide organizational change.

2024 · Canadian Journal for New Scholars in Education

Exploring Trends in BIPOC Student Engagement: A Review of the Literature

A co-authored literature review investigating why student engagement differs between BIPOC students and their White peers in Canadian higher education. The findings point to the importance of faculty representation, on-campus work opportunities, and alternative transit options.
